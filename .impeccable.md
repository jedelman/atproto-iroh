## Design Context

### Users
Small groups coordinating privately without a server anyone has to trust —
deliberately broad, not activist- or cooperative-specific even though the
protocol was originally motivated by that use case (see root README's
"Origin"). Think: a friend group, a family, a book club, a mutual-aid
crew, a small co-op — anyone who wants real coordination (messages,
shared docs, decisions, photos) without handing it to a platform. Not a
technical audience: CLAUDE.md's batteries-included section is explicit
that this targets a non-technical, sometimes tech-hostile user base, so
the UI needs to carry zero required understanding of capabilities,
`did:iroh`, tickets, or P2P mechanics for someone to actually use it day
to day — that machinery should be present for someone who wants to look,
never a prerequisite for someone who doesn't.

### Brand Personality
**Warm and communal.** A shared kitchen table, not a corporate
dashboard — handmade, personal, willing to be a little imperfect on
purpose rather than sanded down to nothing. Warmth over polish. This is
explicitly not "calm fintech trustworthy" and not "sharp utilitarian
infrastructure" — those were live options and were turned down in favor
of something that feels like it was made by people who know each other,
not a platform serving strangers.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Dark default**, deliberately paired with the warm/communal personality
rather than the more common cold-technical reading of dark mode — the
governance/messaging use pattern (checked at odd hours, often on a
phone) favors dark, and the warmth has to come from color temperature
and type choice, not from defaulting to light. Concretely: a dark theme
should lean toward warm neutrals (a hint of brown/amber in the grays,
not blue-black) with a genuinely warm accent hue, not the reflexive
cyan/purple "AI dark mode" palette. No reference sites given — full
creative latitude on specific execution, judged against the "shared
kitchen table" test: does it feel like it was made by someone who cares
about the people using it, not a SaaS trying to look serious.

### Design Principles
1. **Nothing about the protocol should be a prerequisite.** A person
   who never wants to see `did:iroh`, a ticket, or a capability should
   never have to — that stuff exists for the curious, never blocks the
   incurious. Progressive disclosure, not protocol-forward UI.
2. **Warmth over polish, on purpose.** A slightly handmade, imperfect
   texture is a feature here, not a bug to sand away — avoid the
   generic-SaaS smoothness that would undercut "shared kitchen table."
3. **Dark, but warm-dark, not tech-dark.** Reject the reflexive
   cyan-on-charcoal AI palette entirely; warm neutrals and a genuinely
   warm accent color are load-bearing for the brand personality, not a
   detail.
4. **Broad, not activist-coded.** Avoid visual language that reads as
   niche-political or enterprise-technical — this needs to feel usable
   by a family group chat and a mutual-aid crew alike.
5. **"Not a majority vote" has to read as calm, not scary.** The
   governance model (objection-window, ratifies by default) is
   unfamiliar to most people — the UI carries real responsibility for
   making silence-means-consent feel legible and safe, not like a
   loophole.
